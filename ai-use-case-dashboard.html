    Mission

    We turn Consumer Bank's AI capacity into a small number of capabilities that measurably change how customers bank with us and how the business runs.

    Clarity and prioritization are how we get there. One process for every AI idea, so capacity goes to what's worth building — and everything else stops early, cheaply, and on purpose.

    01

    How we operate

    Five commitments that shape every decision in the portfolio.

    01

    Bets, not projects

    Every use case is a hypothesis with a stated expected value and a kill condition. If we can't say what would make us stop, we don't start.

    02

    Evidence before scale

    Pilots exist to answer a question, not to prove we were right. We instrument for the answer up front.

    03

    Finished, not launched

    A use case is done when it sits in a business routine with a named owner and measured value — not when it ships.

    04

    One view, always current

    Anyone can see what's in flight, what it's worth, who owns it, and what it's waiting on — without asking us.

    05

    Speed is a governance outcome

    Risk, Legal, and Model Risk Management engage early and by tier, so controls are the reason we can move — not the reason we can't.

    03

    The lifecycle

    Eight stages, each with one primary outcome, one accountable owner, and one system of record. No initiative advances without satisfying the decision criteria for its current stage.

    Declining early is part of the process — the cost of a bad idea is lowest before anyone builds it. Declined and Transferred outcomes stay in the portfolio as data.

    Triage: every use case leaves with a decision, an owner, and a date

    Approve

    Value is credible; committing capacity now. Needs a named sponsor, no open risk flag, and an agreed value measure.

    Rapid Prototype

    Value plausible but unproven — two weeks, capped, to buy evidence. Returns to triage once, then Approve or Decline.

    More Discovery

    One specific written question blocks the decision. One cycle; a second return forces Approve or Decline.

    Partner

    The capability belongs elsewhere. Warm handoff to a contacted — not assumed — receiving team; closes as Transferred.

    Decline

    Not proceeding — a normal outcome, with a stated reason. The requester hears it directly, not via the portfolio view.

    06

    Where I'd push it next

    The stage-gate creates clarity; agile keeps it honest. Five deliberate moves to make the process faster without giving up control.

    Agile opportunity

    Handle risk up front, prototype fast

    Product, UX, and Engineering review ideas together to separate high-potential opportunities from the rest quickly. Every experiment carries success metrics and exit criteria defined up front, so scale / iterate / pivot / stop is an informed call, not a debate.

    Agile opportunity

    A fast lane for small, low-risk use cases

    Full WSJF scoring and full governance for a small, low-risk pilot is the overhead agile exists to cut. With Business Line Risk able to make a fast risk call at the line, work below a defined size/risk threshold skips from Triage straight to Delivery — no waiting on the monthly Council.

    Agile opportunity

    Governance moves left

    As BLR takes on risk ownership at the line of business, Governance stops being a downstream handoff. BLR expertise embedded in Discovery & Sizing turns governance into a continuous check running alongside the work — not a queue a use case waits in after it's already been prioritized.

    Agile opportunity

    Pull instead of push

    Today a use case only moves on the calendar: weekly triage, monthly council, quarterly planning. A WIP-limited Portfolio Kanban — the model SAFe itself uses — lets anything that clears its bar move the moment it's ready instead of waiting for the next scheduled forum.

    Agile opportunity

    Feed value back continuously

    Benefits Review runs quarterly, so WSJF inputs recalibrate four times a year. Feeding actual adoption and cost data back after every release keeps the scoring model honest in near real time instead of on a lag.

    Next steps

    1. Socialize the intake process
      Walk sponsors and business lines through how ideas enter and what a good submission looks like.
    2. Educate the Bank organization on the process
      Same questions, same scoring, every time — everyone should know the path and the bar.
    3. Bank D&A builds the MS intake form
      A fifteen-minute capture surface; every submission becomes a Jira issue from that point forward.
    4. Use case owners populate intake for top-ranked use cases
      Owners answer only what they can: problem, current process, benefit, data readiness, effort, risk flags.
    5. Bank D&A Tech reviews and prioritizes for experiment
      Context, not gatekeeping — route each submission to Discovery, Rapid Prototype, Partner, or Decline.
